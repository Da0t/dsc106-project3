## 1. Motivation (facts to cite)

* CMIP6: 50+ models from labs around the world [Climate Foresight]
* About 1 in 5 CMIP6 models: ECS of 5 °C or more [Climate Foresight, quoting Hausfather]
  * careful: ECS = long-run warming after CO₂ doubles and the climate settles. We compute **TCR** (warming at the
    moment CO₂ doubles in a 1%-per-year run). Different measure, don't mix the numbers.
* IPCC AR6 TCR: best estimate 1.8 °C, likely 1.4–2.2 °C, very likely 1.2–2.4 °C
  [IPCC AR6 WG1, Chapter 7, section 7.5, Table 7.14]
* Averaging every model overstates future warming. Hausfather et al. (2022): screen out models with TCR outside
  1.4–2.2 °C, or compare models at the same warming level instead of the same year [Hausfather et al. 2022]
  * their point on warming levels: a 2 °C world looks largely the same whenever it arrives (check the article's
    exact wording before quoting it)
* Warming-level framing cuts regional temperature uncertainty a lot, not everywhere [Météo-France]
* Hot models still matter for high-impact risk: more than a 5% chance ECS is above 5 °C (ECS again). The
  problem is how many of them pull the average up [Carbon Brief]
* Counterpoint, worth one honest sentence: an observation-based (econometric) estimate puts TCR at 2.17 °C
  (95% interval 1.72–2.77), above the IPCC's 1.8. "Too hot" is not settled [Storelvmo et al. 2025]
* Why now: record heat in 2023–2024 reopened the question. With those years included, TCR still comes out near
  1.8 K and future warming sits in the low-to-middle part of the model range [Boardman et al. 2026]
* Our angle: reader applies the screen, sees how much the forecast moves, then tests the "same world, sooner"
  idea with the Compare by switch

## 2. Design decisions (reasons + alternatives)

**Whole page**
* one font (Libre Franklin), few sizes; all four charts on the same frame, same margins, same axis style
* one content column: masthead, charts, notes, write-up and footer share both edges; side-by-side charts share
  rows, so they start and end at the same height even when one title wraps
* every view: question as title → one-line "how to read" note → chart → caption with the finding in numbers
  (caption updates as you interact). Each view answers a different question: which models, what it does to the
  forecast, different Arctic or same one sooner, when each model gets to +2 °C
* cut anything that repeated another chart: e.g. an exploratory dot plot of each model's TCR was dropped because
  the scatter already shows every TCR against the same IPCC band; in-chart labels that repeated the note went too
* three controls in one toolbar (Scenario, Models, Compare by); every view follows them. Legend once, in the
  toolbar
* earlier version had more (warming-level picker, three regions, numbered panels, readout boxes, a "things to
  try" list) → busy and inconsistent → cut to three controls, +2 °C and the Arctic only. Good material for
  "alternatives considered" and the development process

**View 1, Which models do you trust? (scatter + brush)**
* two quantities (sensitivity, 2081–2100 warming) → both on position, the most accurate channel
* brush runs along the sensitivity axis because the screen *is* a sensitivity range
* shaded band = IPCC likely range, so the target is visible; presets do it in one click
* fit line and r use all models, not just the kept ones
* alternatives: a checkbox per model (20+ boxes, hides the relationship); a slider (less direct than brushing the
  data); weighting models instead of dropping them (more faithful, much harder to explain at a glance)

**View 2, What does that do to the forecast? (average + band)**
* average of kept models, middle 90% shaded; once you screen, a gray all-models line appears → the effect is a
  visible gap, not a number to remember
* y-axis fixed per scenario, never rescaled by the screen (rescaling would hide the change)
* 2081–2100 window shaded; end labels; crosshair tooltip reads the year

**View 3, A different Arctic, or the same one sooner? (animated axis switch)**
* one line per model. Compare by Year → Warming level moves each line from calendar year to that model's own
  global warming; animated so every line stays the same object and you watch the spread shrink
* by year: marker at the year the *average* model hits +2 °C, bracket = middle-90% spread there. By warming
  level: every model at its own +2 °C. Caption gives both numbers
* only compared when at least two thirds of kept models reach +2 °C (SSP1-2.6 often fails → message instead)
* alternatives: side-by-side small multiples (exploratory graph 6 is the static version); maps at +2 °C (heavy
  data, model-to-model comparison harder on a map)

**View 4, When does each model reach +2 °C? (ranked dot plot)**
* one row per model, most sensitive at top, dot at crossing year → hot models sit top-left
* median lines for "above the range" vs. the rest; separate "after 2100" column for models that never get there
* alternative: histogram of crossing years (loses which model is which, can't link to the other views)

**Color**
* one meaning everywhere: sensitivity (TCR), diverging around the IPCC best estimate 1.8 °C. Blue less
  sensitive, gray near 1.8, red more. Same color for a model in every view
* diverging, not rainbow or single hue: the story is "which side of the assessed value"
* unkept models go gray, never vanish → you see what the screen removed
* checked with a color-vision-deficiency validator (protanopia, deuteranopia); dark mode has its own checked
  colors
* text always neutral ink, never data colors

**Interaction**
* one shared state; hover a model anywhere → highlighted everywhere
* view saved in the URL; data table lists every number (nothing hover-only); Escape clears the selection;
  animations respect reduced-motion

## 3. Data transformations (also on the page)

1. `tas` (monthly near-surface air temperature), one ensemble member per model, five experiments; one grid per
   model across its runs
2. global and Arctic (north of 66.5°N) averages, weighted by cos(latitude)
3. calendar-year means, complete years only
4. TCR: years 61–80 of the 1%-per-year run minus the control run over the matching years (aligned with the
   branch time, linear drift removed)
5. historical + scenario joined, relative to each model's 1850–1900 average
6. 2081–2100 average; 20-year centered running mean; crossing year = middle year of the first 20-year window at
   or above +2 °C
7. "spread" = width of the middle 90% of models

**Limitations:** one ensemble member per model (about ±0.1 °C of noise in each TCR); Arctic box on grids often
100–250 km wide; cos-latitude weights, not exact cell areas; screening is one of several ways to handle hot
models; observation-based TCR estimates disagree (see Storelvmo).
