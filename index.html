Too Hot to Trust?

Climate forecasts average dozens of models, but several of these CMIP6 react to carbon dioxide more strongly than the IPCC considers likely. But do the hot models describe a different world, or the same one sooner?

How to read it. Color is each model's sensitivity: its transient climate response (TCR), how much it warms by the time CO2 doubles. The shaded range is the IPCC's likely range for TCR, 1.4 to 2.2°C. Models you did not keep stay on the charts in gray. Scenarios: SSP1-2.6 low emissions, SSP2-4.5 middle of the road, SSP5-8.5 very high emissions.

Method. TCR is global warming in years 61 to 80 of an idealized run where CO2 rises 1% a year, minus the model's own control run. Temperatures are area-weighted averages of monthly near-surface air temperature, averaged to years, relative to each model's 1850 to 1900 average. The Arctic panel and warming levels use 20-year running means; a model reaches a level in the middle year of the first 20-year period at or above it. Spread is the range covered by the middle 90% of models.

Source. CMIP6 model output via the public Google Cloud archive. We acknowledge the World Climate Research Programme, which coordinated CMIP6, and the climate modeling groups for producing and making available their model output.

Data and methods

All data come from CMIP6, the latest round of the Coupled Model Intercomparison Project, read from the public Google Cloud copy of the archive. We use monthly near-surface air temperature from one ensemble member per model and five experiments: the pre-industrial control run, the idealized 1%-per-year CO2 run, the historical run (1850 to 2014), and the SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5 scenarios (2015 to 2100).

  1. Global and Arctic (north of 66.5°N) averages, weighting each grid cell by the cosine of its latitude.
  2. Calendar-year means of the monthly values, complete years only.
  3. TCR: warming averaged over years 61 to 80 of the 1%-per-year run (CO2 doubles at year 70), minus the control run over the same years after removing its slow drift with a straight-line fit.
  4. Historical and scenario runs joined into one 1850 to 2100 series, relative to the 1850 to 1900 average; end-of-century warming is the 2081 to 2100 average.
  5. Warming levels from a 20-year centered running mean.

The 1.4 to 2.2°C range is the IPCC Sixth Assessment Report's likely range for TCR, the screen proposed by Hausfather et al. (2022). It is a published reference value, not additional data. With one ensemble member per model, natural variability adds about ±0.1°C of noise to each TCR.
